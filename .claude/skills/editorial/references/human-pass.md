# Phases 15–16: Humanity Interview and Human Pass

Router reference for the humanity phase group. Its purpose is to determine
where the draft is technically correct but does not yet sound or feel like
the author. Personality is not synthetically added here — it is
editorially uncovered: what did you actually feel, where does the draft
not sound like you, where were you uncertain, which small observation is
missing, what do you want to make public, which wording would you never
use.

## Phase 15 — Conduct the Humanity Interview

Do not perform the Human Pass solely by rewriting the draft yourself.
Interview the author again, working section by section, a small number of
questions at a time. Record the pass in `content-work/<ID>/humanity-interview.md`
and approved round summaries in `interview-notes.md`.

### Ask where the draft feels emotionally false

- Which part sounds least like you?
- Where does the draft make you sound more certain than you were?
- Where does it make you sound calmer, angrier, or more dramatic than you
  felt?
- Which paragraph is accurate but emotionally empty?
- What did you actually think at that moment?
- What would you say aloud to a trusted colleague?
- Is there a detail you omitted because it seemed too small?
- Was anything funny, awkward, tedious, or irritating?
- Which sentence feels like something a consultant would write rather than
  something you would say?
- Which vulnerability are you comfortable making public?
- Which detail feels too private?
- Where should the article remain impersonal?

Do not pressure the author into revealing personal information — the
author chooses the boundary.

### Recover exact texture

Ask for concrete language:

- What words did you use at the time?
- What did you say to yourself?
- What would your unpolished version of this sentence be?
- What detail did your eye go to first?
- How long did you hesitate?
- What nearly made you stop investigating?
- What did you want the result to mean?
- Which part did you have to check twice?

Use the answers to revise the draft. Do not fabricate inner monologue. Do
not place quotation marks around paraphrases.

### Preserve asymmetry

Not every section needs emotion or first-person narration. Personal detail
should cluster around: the opening scene, moments where interpretation
changed, meaningful trade-offs, and the final decision. Technical
explanation may remain direct and impersonal. A human article is not one
in which every paragraph starts with "I".

## Phase 16 — Perform the Human Pass

After the Humanity Interview, revise the article once for human texture,
using this checklist:

1. Replace abstract passages with approved observed details where useful.
2. Remove phrases the author identified as unnatural.
3. Restore the author's preferred terminology and sentence rhythm.
4. Correct emotional intensity to match the author's account.
5. Add approved uncertainty, hesitation, humor, or frustration where it
   changed the interpretation.
6. Remove invented confidence.
7. Remove invented vulnerability.
8. Remove framework names that do not add reusable value.
9. Combine unnecessarily fragmented one-line paragraphs.
10. Remove repeated rhetorical sentence patterns.
11. Preserve facts, numbers, code, citations, and limitations.
12. Ensure personal details have technical or decision-making relevance.
13. Ensure the opening sounds like a practitioner who noticed something,
    not a keynote speaker performing certainty.
14. Ensure the ending reflects the author's actual decision.
15. Read the text aloud for unnatural cadence.

Do not make the text "more human" by adding: fictional memories, invented
dialogue, exaggerated emotions, fake customer stories, arbitrary jokes, or
rhetorical uncertainty the author did not express.

Humanity comes from accurate specificity. Not decoration.

After the Human Pass, the final evidence check closes the lifecycle:
`references/claims-audit.md`.
